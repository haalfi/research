# Verification upholds the external report, sharpens its edges

The independently produced external report on the first brief holds up. Across five tracks this run made roughly 80 claim checks, several of them overlapping, and found **no claim flatly contradicted**. Its weaknesses sit at the edges. One central citation is wrong: the "2011" privacy-and-workload paper is really two papers, a 2011 workshop paper by Gupta, Miklau & Polyzotis and the full ICDE 2014 paper by Lu, Miklau & Gupta. Several maintenance states are understated (sdcMicro, Anonymeter, PostgreSQL Anonymizer, Jailer) or overstated (Atheris, TestStream as "mature"). It leaves out licence facts that matter for adoption: dbldatagen's Databricks License and the Collate Community License on OpenMetadata's ingestion package. It also understates two disclosure hazards: DataSketches quantile and frequent-item sketches retain raw values, and the NHS England pilot released patient-to-episode fan-out as well as univariate frequencies. What the external report adds is real and now verified. The NHS Artificial Data Pilot is the best-documented *governance* precedent for an aggregates-only boundary. Lu et al. 2014 is the only precedent found that pairs a formal differential-privacy (DP) guarantee with *performance* fidelity. Its cross-source overlap example is arithmetically right, and contingency-table theory explains why. Its "incremental equals full recompute" property is established practice in incremental-view-maintenance engines. Its time-split evaluation proposal is sound, but no one has yet published it for synthetic test data. The run also corrects this repository: PrivLava and PrivPetal have public research code, and ONS Working Paper 16 is dated 15 January 2019. Four earlier conclusions get sharper. Released pairwise overlap counts do not pin how many entities sit in three or more sources. Regenerating test data from a DP characterisation is free, while refreshing the characterisation is not. Delivery behaviour reduces to a short list of per-source process statistics that would need review. Separating overfitting from drift needs a 2×2 evaluation, not one split.

## How to read the evidence in this report

The labels are the notes' own, carried through unchanged:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[D] documented** | A researcher read the full text, the primary page, README, licence file, source file or registry metadata |
| **[T] theoretical** | A mathematical result proven in a cited paper, or checked by this run through exhaustive enumeration |
| **[I] inferred** | Reasoning by the research team or by this synthesis, including all engineering recommendations |
| **[L] lead** | A search extract, abstract-only reading or bare metadata. It is never the basis of a verdict |
| **[U] unreachable** | Tried and not retrieved |
| **[V] vendor** | A project's or company's claim about its own product, including benchmarks the authors ran on their own system |

All sources were accessed on 2026-11-26. Repository dates come from shallow clones of default branches; registry dates come from PyPI, CRAN and Maven Central. The external report is stored verbatim and unverified at `research_notes/Stand in test data cross check/external_report_unverified.md`. Its citations were reduced to bare domain names when it was pasted, so the run could not always tell which page it had read. "The first report" is *Synthetic stand in test data*; "the second report" is *Production relevant test fixtures*. Neither was edited. Commercial products appear only as references for which capabilities exist. Nothing here is legal advice.

Two internal disagreements between this run's tracks were resolved in favour of the stronger evidence. On **PrivLava**, the overlap track relied on the first report's "no packaged code" note and called the external claim unverifiable. The claim-verification track cloned `caicre/PrivLava` and read it [D], so its verdict stands. On **SQLancer**, the claim-verification table says "confirmed" while its takeaway says "partially confirmed". This report uses "partially confirmed", because the README lists only one 2025 approach where the external report says "approaches".

## 2. What the external report adds, and where it falls short

### Six additions survive verification

The external report reached sources neither earlier report used. **First, the NHS England Artificial Data Pilot** is a working public-sector instance of the exact boundary in the brief, now documented down to its code ([page via mirror](https://nhsd-proxy.openprescribing.net/services/artificial-data)) [D]. **Second, Lu, Miklau & Gupta (ICDE 2014)** is the only precedent found that couples a formal privacy guarantee to performance fidelity ([Lu et al. 2014](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~miklau/assets/pubs/dp/lu2014generating.pdf)) [D]. DP "does not offer protection for the population", so where totals such as revenue or customer counts are sensitive, "accurate and private database synthesis is likely to be impossible". **Third, the cross-source overlap example** is correct. ## 3. Pairwise overlap counts leave three-source overlap free and can still pin small cells

### The presence table is the object, and its margins underdetermine it

Every set of cross-source coverage counts is a set of margins of one object. That object is the 2^k presence/absence contingency table of resolved entities: 2^k − 1 observable cells, plus the unobservable "dark" cell of entities in no source. Multiple-systems estimation uses exactly this structure. Its intersection counts are sums of cells, so "the observed data give rise to the 2^t − 1 values" ([Chan, Silverman & Vincent, arXiv 1902.05156](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05156); [Rcapture, JSS 2007](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v019i05)) [D]. The external report's example follows directly. Population 1 has cells AB-only, AC-only and BC-only of 50 each. Population 2 has ABC = 50 and each single-source cell 50. Both give sizes (100, 100, 100) and pairs (50, 50, 50), but unions of 150 and 200 [T]. The bounds on the triple given sizes and pairs are 0 ≤ t ≤ 50, as the Fréchet-type inequalities predict [T].

The caveat the external report misses is about the union. With sizes, pairs **and** the union released, k = 3 is fully determined by inclusion–exclusion [T]. From k = 4 upward it is not. Sizes, pairs and union leave 2^k − 1 − k − C(k,2) − 1 linear degrees of freedom: 0 for k = 3, 4 for k = 4 and 15 for k = 5 [T]. In the run's k = 4 example (sizes 32, all six pairs 14, union 65), exhaustive enumeration found **1,662** distinct consistent tables. The four-way overlap ranged over 0–7 and each "exactly three sources" cell over 0–10 [T, checked by enumeration]. Log-linear MSE resolves this the same way every time: it *assumes* third- and higher-order interactions are zero, because "without placing constraints on the αθ parameters, the model is not identifiable" ([Chan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05156)) [D]. A generator calibrated to pairwise counts therefore picks one table from a feasible family. The candidates include the maximum-entropy table and the pairwise-log-linear one. Reconciliation and deduplication workloads depend on how many entities sit in three or more sources, so that choice is a fidelity decision, not a detail [I].

### Bounds make small cells disclosive even when only margins leave

The same theory governs disclosure. Given released margins, every cell has sharp integer lower and upper bounds, and "if a cell count is small and the upper bound is 'close' to the lower bound, the intruder knows with certainty that there is only a small number of individuals possessing the characteristics corresponding to the cell" ([Dobra & Fienberg, PNAS 2000](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC17264)) [D]. Closed forms exist when the margins are the sufficient statistics of a decomposable log-linear model: the upper bound is the minimum over clique margins, and the lower bound is max(0, Σ clique margins − Σ separator margins). They also exist for dichotomous k-way tables with all (k − 1)-way margins fixed. That is exactly the shape of a presence table ([Dobra, Karr & Sanil 2003](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/adobra/Files/Papers/ADstatcomp2003.pdf)) [D]. Releasing *all* pairwise overlaps for k ≥ 3 forms a cycle, which is non-decomposable. It then needs the generalised shuttle algorithm, integer programming or, for small k, enumeration [I].

Sparse data are where this bites. In a k = 4 example with sizes 10, pairs (5, 3, 2, 2, 3, 5) and union 26, **exactly one table is consistent**. It discloses "exactly in sources 1 and 3: 1 entity" and "in all four: 2", although every released number looks aggregate [T]. Dobra, Karr & Sanil report the same phenomenon: a 2×2×2×2 table uniquely determined by its six two-way margins [D]. Their example also shows why the agency tools are not enough on their own. Linear-programming bounds there were fractional or non-sharp for every cell [D]. sdcTable's `attack()` (0.34.1, 2026-11-12) solves continuous LPs with HiGHS and has no integrality setting in its source ([CRAN sdcTable](https://cran.r-project.org/package=sdcTable)) [D; LP reading I], and τ-Argus's audit also uses an external LP solver ([τ-Argus manual](https://julienjamme.github.io/manual_tau_argus_to_quarto/03_A_tour_of_tauargus.html)) [D]. Both audit *suppression patterns*, not arbitrary sets of intersection counts. LP bounds can be wider than the sharp integer bounds, which means they can understate disclosure [I]. No maintained standalone package implementing the generalised shuttle was found [absence of evidence].

### Under DP, the full table is cheaper than its margins

With the resolved entity as the privacy unit, each entity falls in exactly one presence cell. The full 2^k − 1 table therefore has L1 sensitivity 1, and every size, intersection and union can be computed from the noisy table as post-processing at no further cost [T, standard DP sensitivity; I as recommendation]. Releasing all pairwise counts directly costs up to C(k,2) in sensitivity, because an entity in m sources touches C(m,2) of them [T]. For small k (say up to 10, i.e. 1,023 cells) the noisy full table is the better release. For larger k, the presence bits can be treated as k binary attributes of the root entity record. Chosen margins are then measured with MST or AIM selection and fitted with `mbi`, which reduces cross-source overlap to ordinary marginal synthesis [I]. DPSynth's tree-only relational module fits an Entity → per-source-record schema, with privacy "protected at the root parent record". Whether it models correlation between sibling child tables was not verified, so putting presence bits on the root is the simpler route ([DPSynth relational README](https://github.com/google-parfait/dpsynth)) [D; I].

## 4. Delivery testing has executors and oracles, but still no released fault generator

### The four named harnesses execute scripts; none generates delivery faults

The external report implies that TestStream, Flink DataGen, the Flink harnesses and SimPy jointly cover delivery behaviour. They do not [I]. TestStream is a precise **script player**. It replays a fully specified interleaving of elements, watermark advances and processing-time advances, and can show "that late-arriving data produces refinements when it arrives before the maximum allowed lateness, and is dropped after" ([Beam blog, 2016-10-20](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apache/beam/master/website/www/site/content/en/blog/test-stream.md)) [D]. It has no notion of a delivery file, source calendar, duplicate rate or correction [I, from the API]. Beam's own "Test Your Pipeline" guide never mentions it ([test-your-pipeline.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apache/beam/master/website/www/site/content/en/documentation/pipelines/test-your-pipeline.md)) [D, documented absence]. Its strongest defect evidence is against **runners**. Prism admitted data for a window already past allowed lateness, reproduced with a four-event script and fixed on 2026-10-26 ([#40818](https://github.com/apache/beam/issues/40818)) [D]. No documented case of TestStream finding a bug in a *user* pipeline was found [absence of evidence].

Flink's operator harnesses give the same exact watermark and timer control at operator level (`processWatermark`, `setProcessingTime`). The docs warn they "are currently not part of the public API" ([Flink testing docs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apache/flink/master/docs/content/docs/dev/datastream/testing.md)) [D]. DataGen generates values, not delivery faults. Lateness can only be approximated with `max-past`, a random backdating relative to now [D; I]. SimPy is a general discrete-event kernel whose machine-shop pattern maps naturally onto source outages, but it ships no data-delivery library [D; I]. salabim (26.0.9, 2026-11-03) and Ciw (3.2.7, 2025-12-05) are MIT-licensed alternatives, checked at metadata level only ([PyPI salabim](https://pypi.org/pypi/salabim/json)) [D]. The one documented delivery-schedule design is **ArrivalBench** ([arXiv 2610.02363v2, 19 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02363)) [D]. Its schedules partition a logical event log into delivery batches, "possibly duplicating events, reordering them, withholding and later releasing them, or aborting mid-batch". The pipeline's final state "must equal a batch recomputation of the complete logical log under every schedule". It covers six hazards: late arrival, out-of-order change data, duplicate delivery, crash-and-retry, backfill and additive schema evolution. Two benign controls separate incrementality bugs from temporal ones, and "the same (task, schedule, seed, artifact) yields byte-identical verdicts". The result is stark. Single-execution grading certified 86–100% of agent-written pipelines; replay grading showed **7.0–79.2% silent failure**. But it has 40 DuckDB tasks by one author, and the harness "will be released with the archival version" [D].

### Recompute-equals-incremental is well documented and finds real bugs

The oracle has a strong record in incremental-view-maintenance engines. Databricks' Enzyme creates a materialised view for each random query, applies randomised changes, and "validates that the incremental refresh produces identical results to complete recomputation". It adds production A/B checks that catch issues "under specific data distributions or timing conditions not covered by synthetic tests" ([arXiv 2603.27775v2, 30 Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27775)) [D; vendor-authored, no bug counts]. pg_trickle's shadow mode runs differential and full refresh each cycle and compares them as multisets at "roughly double" cost. It lists four delta-rule bug classes found this way, including NULL→non-NULL group-key transitions and zero-count groups left behind in some joins ([pg_trickle blog](https://api.pgxn.org/src/pg_trickle/pg_trickle-0.83.0/blog/shadow-mode-correctness-fuzzing.md)) [D/V]. Materialize's sqllogictest runner re-executes each SELECT through an indexed view and flags `InconsistentViewOutcome` ([runner.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MaterializeInc/materialize/main/src/sqllogictest/src/runner.rs)) [D]. That checks initial hydration, which is weaker than deltas applied over time [I]. RisingWave claims "nearly 40 bugs" from batch-versus-streaming SQLsmith runs ([RisingWave blog, 2023-08-08](https://risingwave.com/blog/how-randomized-sql-testing-can-help-detect-bugs/)) [V]. SQLxDiff found 57 previously unknown bugs across four systems, 4 of them in RisingWave ([arXiv 2501.01236](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.01236)) [D]. Holey found 10 wrong-result bugs in Flink 2.3.0, but in batch mode, excluding streaming by design. It is no evidence about watermark semantics ([arXiv 2606.11132v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11132)) [D].

At the framework level, dbt unit tests can mock `is_incremental` to test both modes. But "there is currently no way to unit test whether the dbt framework inserted/merged the records into your existing model correctly" ([dbt unit tests](https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/build/unit-tests)) [D]. Searches for 2022–2026 work that fuzzes Flink, Kafka Streams or Spark Structured Streaming for watermark or late-data bugs found none [absence of evidence]. Two caveats apply to the oracle [I]. It needs a specification of the correct final state under late or corrected data: ArrivalBench grades "a watermark that drops late data" as a failure, so a platform with deliberate cut-offs needs a reference that applies the same cut-offs. And a single in-order batch is the benign control that tells an incrementality bug from a temporal one.

## 5. A single time split confounds overfitting with drift

### Held-out workloads are established; later-window evaluation of generators is not

Holding out queries is established in workload-aware generation and in learned cardinality estimation. SAM separates "fidelity" (Q-error on its input constraints) from "closeness" on "an independent test query set". For IMDB, it trains on the 100K MSCN queries of up to three relations and tests on JOB-light, with up to five ([SAM, SIGMOD 2022](https://jamesyang2333.github.io/files/SAM_Camera_Ready_v1.pdf)) [D]. Even the best method kept a heavy tail there: median Q-error 2.29 but 90th percentile 27.78 and a maximum of 2·10^5 [D, author benchmark V]. MSCN's 95th-percentile q-error rose from 7.66 to 2,397 on four-join queries never seen in training. Its authors concede that "generalizing to queries that are not in the vicinity of the training data remains challenging" ([arXiv 1809.00677](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00677)) [D].

Drift is tested separately. Wang et al. appended 20% of rows with deliberately different correlations: "Learned methods cannot catch up with fast data updates", while DBMS estimators stayed stable ([arXiv 2012.06743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.06743)) [D]. Negi et al. trained on IMDb-1950 and tested on full IMDb. Standard MSCN "gets much worse than PostgreSQL", and their robust variant never did ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10495282)) [D]. None of the generator papers read evaluates a generated database against a *later production window*. Their held-out sets come from the same snapshot [absence of evidence].

Official statistics has the closest analogues. The Census/Cornell Synthetic Data Server validated users' *unplanned* analyses on confidential data with disclosure review. Agreement was often poor (SynLBD interval overlap: median 0, mean 0.206), and uptake was thin: 5 of 79 SynLBD projects. A real income-share discontinuity at 50% had "No such break in the synthetic data" ([Vilhuber & Abowd 2016](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0d52578a-0a7f-4eff-8be1-83cc9d1bfffa/content)) [D]. The NIST 2018–19 DP challenge scored methods on other years and states than those used for development: Match 1 trained on 2017 fire calls and scored on 2016, Match 3 trained on Colorado and scored on Arizona and Vermont. Yet "the competitors could modify their approach based on the scoring metrics" announced in advance ([Bowen & Snoke](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.12704)) [D]. Outside data synthesis, time-respecting evaluation is a rule. TRIPOD calls random splits "a weak and inefficient form of internal validation", and temporal validation "intermediate between internal and external" ([TRIPOD E&E, 2015](https://legacyfileshare.elsevier.com/promis_misc/XKME_TRIPOD_Guidelines_moreinfo.pdf)) [D]. TESSERACT showed that time-inconsistent splits inflate results, with performance dropping "up to 50% in practice" ([arXiv 1807.07838](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.07838)) [D]. Bangash et al. found conclusions "not stable across different periods of time" ([arXiv 1911.06348](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.06348)) [D]. Facebook's predictive test selection evaluates on the most recent week, retrains weekly behind an assertion gate, and pays for ground truth with full "learning test runs" on about a quarter of changes ([arXiv 1810.05286](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05286)) [D].

### What the proposal establishes, and what it cannot

Held-out workloads and a later window do establish two things. One is whether fidelity on non-calibrated workloads matches fidelity on calibrated ones, which measures overfitting. The other is how fast fidelity decays from window t to t+1, which gives a renewal interval [I]. They do not establish fidelity for workloads absent from both sets, behaviour under drift larger than the observed t→t+1 change, detection of unknown fault classes, or correctness as opposed to behavioural similarity [I]. Two distinct cross-period designs also need separating [I]. *Method transfer* re-runs calibration on window t+1, as NIST did, and tests the procedure. *Artefact staleness* keeps data generated from window t and scores it against window t+1, and tests how fast a released characterisation goes stale. The external report's wording reads as the second.

## 6. Four earlier conclusions sharpen, none reverses, and the precedents find their place

Everything in this section is engineering inference [I] built on the documented findings above. None of it is a tested design.

**Which overlap counts to release, and what they disclose.** The first report said cross-source overlap could be exported as "overlap matrices and lag histograms". That is now too loose. Pairwise matrices fix only the pairwise structure. With the union they pin three sources; for four or more they leave higher-order overlap to an assumption. The defensible releases are two. One is the pairwise-MSE sufficient statistics (sizes, all pairwise intersections, total distinct), shipped with an explicit statement that 3+-source overlap is model-implied. The other is the full presence table, or chosen higher-order margins, where reconciliation fidelity depends on them. Under DP, the noisy full table is the cheaper release for small k. Without DP, every proposed release needs a sharp *integer*-bounds audit of all 2^k cells. That audit must be cumulative over all earlier releases, and it must flag cells pinned below the threshold (for example 1–9) or pinned at zero beside a small positive margin. Rare patterns can be collapsed (for example into "≥ 3 other sources"). Because sources arrive with different delays, each presence table must carry an as-of date and a linkage version. Coverage-and-delay counts such as "entities in A appearing in B within d days" are further margins of a time-extended table and enter the same audit. A fitted MSE model also yields a dark-figure estimate: latent entities that a new or late source could reveal. Where a source is licensed, its size and overlaps may themselves be licence-restricted figures, independent of privacy.

**Characterisation refresh and regeneration are separate operations.** The external report's §4.3 and the first report's "one DP characterisation per schema version" agree. Regenerating any number of datasets from a released DP characterisation is post-processing and costs nothing. Refreshing the characterisation is a new measurement: it spends budget and needs a new disclosure review. The precedents add three sharpenings. First, the NHS pilot shows what one review cycle costs in practice: a human checklist review and senior sign-off per release. Second, Lu et al. warn that DP protects individuals, not population totals. Where table sizes or business volumes are themselves confidential, DP noise alone does not make them releasable. Third, contribution bounds (Lu et al.'s K, PrivLava's τ at the 99th percentile of group sizes, DPSynth's `max_children_per_parent`) leave high-fan-out entities only partially protected, and the bound itself is a sensitive parameter. This refines the first report's point that the privacy unit must be the resolved entity: the platform's heaviest entities are exactly the ones a fan-out bound truncates. They are therefore best served by the external report's "independently designed stress cases" rather than by released statistics.

**Delivery-schedule statistics that would leave.** None of the harnesses needs real rows. What the protected environment would export is a small set of delivery-process parameters per source or source class, extending the first report's Layer 2 list. The delivery calendar covers nominal cadence, inter-arrival jitter, and day-of-week and holiday effects. Lateness covers quantiles of delivery time minus reference time, tail weight (p95, p99) and the share beyond any platform cut-off. The out-of-order rate comes with its version-gap distribution. Duplicates split into whole-file re-delivery rate, partial-batch overlap, within-batch versus cross-batch duplicate rates (ArrivalBench treats them as distinct hazards) and the time to re-delivery. Corrections need a rate, a back-dating depth and a reversal-versus-amendment split. Gaps and outages need a missing-delivery rate, outage durations (an MTTF/MTTR analogue) and burst or backfill size after outages. Volume needs records per delivery and a trend. Crash and partial-load rates and schema-event rates complete the list. These are process statistics, not entity values. But per-source parameters for a few identifiable providers can be commercially sensitive, and extreme quantiles over small counts can reveal individual events, so they need coarsening, grouping, thresholds or DP. The oracle side needs no export of data. A recompute-equals-incremental check can run inside on real deliveries, pg_trickle-style, and export only pass/fail and divergence counts. The resulting chain is a delivery generator (SimPy-style or custom) emitting an event log, which becomes the script for TestStream, a Flink harness or a batch-replay harness, checked against a recompute that applies the platform's own cut-off rules. That chain is assembled here; no source documents it end to end.

**A 2×2 design separates overfitting from drift.** A single "calibrate on t, test independent workloads on t+1" cell cannot tell overfitting from drift. Crossing workload set (calibration versus held-out) with window (t versus t+1) does. Calibration workloads in t+1 isolate drift, and held-out workloads in t isolate overfitting. The held-out workloads should use templates, join graphs or columns absent from calibration, because fresh constants on the same templates mostly test interpolation (Negi et al.'s case a). They should be fixed before calibration and withheld from whoever tunes the generator; NIST's announced metrics show the leak otherwise. Walk-forward over several windows, summarised across windows in the style of TESSERACT's AUT, should replace a single split. Ground-truth runs inside the boundary must be budgeted, as Facebook budgeted its learning runs. Each window's exported metrics also add to cumulative disclosure. This extends the first report's drift detection, which detects *change*, with a direct measure of whether a generated artefact is still *fit for purpose*.

**Where the precedents sit.** The NHS pilot is the operational and governance template: one dataset per scope, aggregates only, suppression plus rounding, human review, a physical scope move, a generator that cannot read source data, explicit limitation notices, and published 10k and 1M sizes. Lu et al. is the methodological template for performance fidelity under a formal guarantee. Simulacrum sits between them, with reviewed multi-dimensional cubes of at least 50 patients. OpenSAFELY sits at the record-free end. Lu et al.'s citation chain is small: OpenAlex counts 27 citing works for ICDE 2014 and 8 for AMW 2011 ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W2028628865)) [L]. It leads into DP relational synthesis for answer accuracy, such as PrivLava, which calls Lu et al. "inapplicable" because it preserves query cost, not answers ([arXiv 2304.04545](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.04545)) [D]. It also leads into DP SQL engines. Touchstone and SAM do not cite it ([Touchstone](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/atc18/atc18-li-yuming.pdf); [SAM](https://jamesyang2333.github.io/files/SAM_Camera_Ready_v1.pdf)) [D, checked for these two papers only]. No work found combines DP release with plan-fidelity generation for testing. Combining NHS-style governance with Lu-style DP workload statistics, fed to a Touchstone-, SAM- or DBRepro-style generator, is an integration gap rather than a missing component.

## Conclusion

Reading the external report against primary sources changes the shape of the problem more than its answer. The earlier reports treated "release some cross-source aggregates" and "simulate deliveries" as open building tasks. The overlap theory turns the first into a precise, auditable question: which margins of a 2^k table to release, under what modelling assumption for the cells left free, and checked by sharp integer bounds cumulatively across releases, or else the full table under entity-level DP. The delivery work turns the second into a clean division of labour. Executors (TestStream, Flink harnesses) and oracles (recompute equals incremental) are mature enough to adopt. The missing piece is a schedule generator, and ArrivalBench shows its shape. Its 7–79% silent-failure rate under replay suggests that pipelines tested only on in-order data are the norm, not the exception.

The cross-check also sets a standard for using outside syntheses. A well-argued report can be right on substance and still wrong on the facts a team acts on: whose paper it is, whether a tool is maintained, under which licence, and what its artefacts retain. The external report's architecture largely converges with this repository's: characterise inside, review, generate outside, validate inside. That convergence is the strongest evidence that the pattern is sound. Its unverified tool table, by contrast, would have led to adopting a non-OSI generator and an ingestion framework under a community licence, and to exporting sketches that contain real values.
