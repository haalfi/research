## 1. Kernsatz

Nothing structural changes. The check map, the stopping rule and the "leads,
not verdicts" stance on LLM judges all stand. One design detail is now backed
by measurement: an agent that restates or splits a Decision must not see the
dialog, because in claimcheck a restater that saw its source lost 6.0 to 8.8
points of defect recall. The leads' two strongest-sounding claims did not hold
in full text: RAG4C's "0.97 inconsistency accuracy" is a synonym-grouping
score, and query-conditioned NLI reached at most 0.68 balanced accuracy on
document inconsistency.

## 3. Changed findings

*Inference* marks the reasoning that carries a measured result over to the
record.

**Change 1. T3, the claim-to-span design: one step now has a measured
mechanism.**

- Old: "**Claim-to-span alignment** (own proposal): split the Decision into
  atomic claims; each needs a span in the answer or in the option the answer
  chose" (earlier report, § 2 T3).
- New: the agent that splits a Decision into atomic claims **does not see
  the dialog**. The judge then compares each claim with the answer, and sees
  neither the whole Decision nor anything else of the record.
- Reason: in claimcheck, a restater shown the source lost 6.0 to 8.8 points
  of defect recall, and a judge shown the derived artefact raised more false
  alarms (L1).
- Caveats, *Inference*: the evidence comes from short formal statements
  with mutation defects. Whether the effect carries over to prose decisions
  and to implied additions is unmeasured.

**Change 2. T3, the LLM-judge row: the "weak judges" evidence is dated, but
the verdict stays.**

- Old: "an LLM fidelity judge yields leads, not verdicts" (earlier report,
  § 2 T3), resting on 2023–24 figures with balanced accuracy of 50 to 70.
- New: in 2026, one call scores 91 to 95 balanced accuracy on short formal
  statements (L1, gemini-3.7-flash).
- The stance stays, because:
  - claimcheck reports no precision;
  - its defects are dominated by negation, the easiest kind;
  - its authors name long, cross-referenced artefacts as outside what they
    measured. A record is exactly such an artefact.
- The earlier report's debt item 7 ("2023-24 models") is answered for short
  formal statements only.

**Change 3. T4, the independence assumption: from own trial to adjacent
evidence.**

- Old: "Same-model readers given one brief are positively dependent, which
  inflates the overlap and biases N̂ down" (earlier report, § 2 T4,
  *Inference* backed by the own trial).
- New: across 7 models, mean pairwise error correlation is 0.285, so seven
  models carry the information of about 2.6 independent ones, and three
  small models of about 1.8 (L2).
- Caveats: the data are multiple-choice answers, and N_eff is a heuristic.
  The dependence claim now has adjacent measured support. **The stopping
  rule does not change**, since it already avoids capture-recapture below 4
  captures and estimates within a kind.

**Change 4. T1, adjacent evidence for agent-run elicitation and checklist
gap-finding.**

- Old: "No surviving source measures a technique that surfaces questions
  **no** checklist raises" (earlier report, § 2 T1).
- **That statement stands.** Neither L3 nor L6 counts findings outside its
  reference set or its fixed dimensions.
- Added as adjacent evidence:
  - An LLM interviewer left 26 to 39% of an author-built requirement set
    unelicited (L3).
  - An LLM applying a closed 10-dimension checklist recalled 79.5 to 98.4%
    of a production-derived reference, at 46 to 79% precision (L6).
- *Inference*: the second supports the slot method, an agent filling a
  fixed grid, as high-recall and low-precision. Its findings need a judged
  triage step, which the check map already has.

**Change 5. T2, NLI: disconfirming search (b) is strengthened; no
replacement.**

- Old: the pairwise NLI row (Fazelnia et al., F1 22 to 55%) and the
  conclusion that pairwise detectors are weak and miss composite conflicts.
- New: query-conditioned NLI with 2024 models reached at most 0.681
  balanced accuracy on document inconsistency, and it is strictly pairwise
  (L4).
- It is not a stronger replacement, as the lead suggested. Conditioning on
  the claim under test is already what the typed register does mechanically
  for Closed × Member.

**Change 6. § 5 debt item 1 (extraction error): one weak measurement now
exists.**

- Old: "No source measured what LLM extraction into logic or tables
  introduces or hides."
- New: one tool's authors measured LLM extraction at P 0.99, R 0.98 (L5).
  That was on synthetic, LLM-generated requirements, with every document
  inconsistent by construction.
- The debt stands. claimcheck adds a **design** for validating an
  extraction: restate each extracted claim in English, blind to the record,
  and judge it against the record text (L1). It complements dual extraction,
  and its error rate on extraction is unmeasured.

## 4. Revised verification debt

The order changes, and so does item 3.

1. **Dual extraction of OP-01, plus blind restatement of each extracted
   claim.** Measure both against O14 to O44. This is unchanged as the top
   debt; claimcheck adds the second validation design.
2. **The typed claim register on OP-01 at `c58ee74`.** Unchanged.
3. **Claim-to-span fidelity, with a blind splitter and a judge that sees
   only the claim and the answer (change 1), on OP-01's class 2 items** (O14
   to O16, O31, O33, O36, O37). This is new: it tests whether claimcheck's
   mechanism carries over to prose decisions and implied additions.
4. **Seeded-defect calibration per reviewer kind.** Unchanged.
5. **Error correlation between LLM *reviewers* on one record.** The
   L2 figure comes from multiple-choice answers. Debt: compute the pairwise
   correlation of findings between same-model and cross-model readers on
   OP-01, and compare it with L2's 0.285.
6. **Class 4 discovery.** Still unmeasured. L3's "context-enhancing"
   questions (10 to 15% of an LLM interviewer's questions) hint at where to
   look, but nobody counted them against an oracle.
