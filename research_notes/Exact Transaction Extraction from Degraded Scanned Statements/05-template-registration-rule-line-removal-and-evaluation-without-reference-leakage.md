# Track 5: Template registration, rule-line removal and evaluation without reference leakage

Round 1 of the deep-research workflow, 2026-11-21. One reader agent per source extracted up to five claims with a supporting quote; the 25 highest-ranked claims across all tracks then went to three adversarial verifiers each (two refutations kill a claim).
Claims marked "not voted" are leads until verified; see the round 2 notes.

Search query: `form registration template alignment scanned documents table line removal morphological Hough preserve characters touching lines OCR evaluation ground truth degraded scan synthetic degradation print-scan held-out overfitting`

Rationale: Layout generalization to gap months: reusable column templates from the issuer layout, anchor-based registration/dewarping, line removal that does not damage touching glyphs, and evaluation design (print-and-rescan or synthetic degradation of other electronic months, held-out splits, exact-field/row metrics) that avoids tuning on the single paired document.

## S16: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/IR/nistir5687.pdf

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 1995-07.

- **S16.0** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Template/mask-based form removal (identify form, register image to a stored blank-form mask, erase mask pixels) destroys any character pixels that overlap form lines, leaving holes or clipped strokes; it also depends on strict form reproduction and on registration. Garris states the repair of such holes is harder than detecting them. (Source is on binary 200-300 dpi handprint forms; transfer to machine-printed statement rows with minus signs/commas/underscores touching rules is inference.)
  - Quote: "Previous techniques would simply erase the line, typically by applying a mask or template of the form. If characters overlapped the form information, this data was also erased, leaving gaping holes in the middle of character strokes (especially descenders). [...] The challenge is not so much in the detection of holes, but rather in determining how the holes should be accurately filled."
- **S16.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Rule lines can be detected and removed without a stored blank-form mask, without precise field coordinates and without deskewing: a Hough line transform restricted to +/-5 degrees (1 degree steps) finds dominant horizontal lines in a loosely zoned sub-image; line width is estimated as the median height of 1-pixel-wide vertical slices along the line, slices <= median are erased, and remaining slices are handled by tolerance rules, void filling and corner clipping. Vertical lines were removed by rotating 90 degrees with tighter parameters (1 degree, 98% black-pixel coverage). Only coarse global registration is needed, so the method uses only information present in the scan itself.
  - Quote: "Some global registration of the form is needed to produce loosely defined zones, but no precise pixel coordinates of the field's size and location are required and no prestored image mask of the form is required. In this way, a form's geometry is still needed, but the dependence on this information has been greatly relaxed."
- **S16.2** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Replacing mask-based erasure with stroke-preserving line removal raised character accuracy from 76.9% to 80.1% on 54,340 handprinted lowercase letters (2,090 fields, NIST SD19), but only after the classifier was retrained on character images produced by the new removal method (12,300 verified characters, under 8 person-hours). The result is for handprint with a 1995 KL/PNN classifier, not machine print; magnitude is not transferable to printed statements.
  - Quote: "The old system achieved a character output accuracy of 76.9% out of a possible 54,340 lowercase letters; the test actually consisted of 2,090 lowercase fields. The new system achieved a character output accuracy of 80.1% on the same characters."
- **S16.3** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A net accuracy gain from a preprocessing change concealed statistically significant new errors: comparing per-confusion-pair error distributions over 10 test partitions with a Student's t test (p < 2%) found 41 of 650 confusion pairs changed significantly, 30 improved and 11 worsened (e.g. q->g rose from a mean of 18.9 to 25.1 errors per 209-character partition; also j->v, r->g). Garris argues global metrics alone are insufficient to judge a pipeline modification and that per-confusion-pair paired comparison is needed.
  - Quote: "The bottom portion of the table lists all those confusion pairs in which the new system did worse than the old system. [...] These represent the significant trade-offs of introducing the new form removal method into the recognition system. [...] This analysis is very useful because both the improvements and the losses in recognition performance are determined, whereas global performance statistics only report the net change in performance."
- **S16.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The stroke-preserving line removal has documented failure modes relevant to printed tables: (a) the dashed-line detector mistakes machine-printed text for dashed lines and partially erases it; (b) strokes thinner than the rule and enveloped by it are cut in two, with ambiguity increasing as stroke width decreases; (c) lines longer than about 500 px at 300 dpi are not straight enough for a single Hough fit and require tiling; (d) the input is binary, so a black pixel may be line, character or both.
  - Quote: "The dashed line detection does confuse machine print with dashed lines because the statistics of the black and white pixel runs are very regular and uniform. This confusion results in extra dashed lines being detected at the very tops and bottoms of the images (places where there is residual machine printed text), and the machine print is partially erased."

## S17: https://www.haralick.org/conferences/automatic_generation_character_groundtruth_closed_loop.pdf

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 1996-08 (Proceedings of ICPR '96, IEEE, pp. 669-675).

- **S17.0** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A single global similarity, affine or projective (perspective) transform does not accurately register an ideal electronic page image to its printed/photocopied/scanned counterpart; the residual displacement is non-linear and not systematic across the page. (Implication for this task, inference: a global template registration of a statement scan to a layout template or to the paired electronic original will leave local column/row offsets that a positional parser must tolerate or correct locally.)
  - Quote: "Unfortunately, none of the three geometric transformations described in sections 4 and 5 model the transformation very accurately. That is, the real points are displaced from ideal transformed points in some nonlinear fashion. The mismatch must arise from nonlinearities in the optical and mechanical systems. [...] We see that there is a lot of error. This error is not systematic over the entire page."
- **S17.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): In a controlled calibration experiment (a 23 x 30 grid of '+' marks on a 300 dpi, 3300 x 2500 px page, printed and scanned, projective transform estimated from four corner feature points), the residual registration error after projective registration was within about +/-4 pixels per coordinate, and the authors therefore used a +/-7 pixel local search window. (The PDF text layer is itself OCR'd: '±4' is rendered as 'f4' and '±4' as '314'; the +/-4 reading is confirmed by the stated -7..7 window.)
  - Quote: "Next, for each calibration point we compute the registration error vector, which is the displacement vector between the real calibration point and the projected calibration point. The maximum error we attain is with[in] ±4 pixels in each coordinate. [...] Since the maximum error in the registration is ±4 pixels, we used a window with -7 <= Δx, Δy <= 7."
- **S17.2** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Residual local non-linearity can be removed by a two-stage registration: global perspective transform followed by per-character local translation search using Hamming-distance template matching against the scanned image; the authors report the resulting character bounding-box groundtruth is accurate to within one pixel. This is a method for transferring labels/boxes from an electronic original onto a degraded scan of the same page, i.e. it requires the paired original and is suitable for building evaluation/training groundtruth, not for extracting gap months.
  - Quote: "Next, each character in the perspective transformed image is locally translated and matched (using Hamming distance) with the same size subimage in the scanned document image. [...] The size of the search window is decided by the calibration experiment. If the error vectors are large, the search window has to be made large. This local search process gives us a highly accurate groundtruth, and the errors are within a pixel."
- **S17.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Local template matching introduces its own failure mode: with a large search window, a character can be matched to an adjacent visually similar character (e.g. 'i' vs 'i' or 'i' vs 'l'); the authors detect and correct such outliers by robust regression of a piecewise bilinear function over the local error vectors, assuming displacement varies slowly within a small area.
  - Quote: "At times, when two very similar characters (for example two 'i's, or one 'i' and one 'l') are close to each other, the template matching process can match the perspective transformed character to the wrong scanned character. This typically happens if we use a large search window size. [...] We use robust regression for detecting and correcting such outliers. [...] we fit a piecewise bilinear function to the x and y translation errors associated with characters in a small area of the image."
- **S17.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The method was applied to 33 English pages (over 62,000 symbols) at roughly 5 minutes per page on a SUN Sparc 10, plus Hindi and FAXed documents without modification; the authors state three limitations: sensitivity to the feature points used for registration, poor performance on bound-book scans, and that print-and-scan documents are only a subset of real-world document images (a representativeness caveat for any evaluation set built by re-degrading an electronic original).
  - Quote: "Few of the limitations of our algorithm are: (i) it is sensitive to the feature points that are used for registration, (ii) if the scanned image is from a bound book, our procedure will not perform well, (iii) the population of documents one can generate by printing and scanning ideal documents is a subset of the population of document images in the real world."

## S19: https://huggingface.co/papers/2208.14558

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2022-08-30 (arXiv v1); revised 2023-03-24 (v2); published at ICDAR 2023.

- **S19.0** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Augraphy is an open-source (MIT) Python library that synthesizes scan/photocopy/fax-style degradations of clean document images, with 26 built-in augmentations composable across three pipeline phases plus a paper-background step. For this task it is a way to produce degraded test scans from born-digital pages whose ground truth is known, without touching the paired real scan. (The applicability to statements is inference; the paper does not test financial tables.)
  - Quote: "Augraphy offers 26 individual augmentation methods out-of-the-box across three “phases” of augmentations, and these individual phase augmentations can be composed together along with a “paper factory” step where different paper backgrounds can be added to the augmented image."
- **S19.1** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): On 15 born-digital documents, Tesseract word accuracy on Augraphy-degraded versions was on average 52% lower than on the clean versions (up to 84% lower), while the pages remained human-readable. Caveats from the full text: n=15, the reference labels were Tesseract's own output on the clean images rather than the true strings, the pipeline settings are not given in the paper body, and the metric is word accuracy, not exact-field accuracy.
  - Quote: "We compared the word accuracy rate on the noisy OCR results versus the ground-truth noise-free OCR results, and found that the noisy OCR results were on average 52% less accurate, with a range of up to 84%."
- **S19.2** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The authors present synthetic Augraphy degradation as a valid method for building challenging OCR evaluation sets from clean documents with known text, i.e. evaluation data that does not require a real degraded scan paired with an electronic original.
  - Quote: "This example use-case demonstrates the effectiveness of using Augraphy to create challenging test data that remains human readable suitable for evaluating OCR systems."
- **S19.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A learned denoiser (NAFNet) trained on Augraphy-degraded pages (ShabbyPages: 6,202 pages at 150 ppi) and tested on physically degraded out-of-distribution pages (ShabbyReal) reached only SSIM 0.71 / PSNR 32.52 and visibly altered glyphs by thickening strokes. This is a documented preprocessing-artifact failure mode of learned denoising; the paper reports no OCR accuracy after denoising, so any benefit or harm to exact-field OCR is untested.
  - Quote: "The SSIM score indicates that these models generalize very well, though they do overcompensate for shadowing features around text, by increasing the line thickness in the prediction, resulting in a bold font."
- **S19.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The paper's evidence that Augraphy output is 'realistic' is qualitative: a single visual comparison against one real noisy RVL-CDIP page (Figure 1). No quantitative comparison of synthetic versus real degradation (e.g. matching OCR error distributions) is reported, so transfer of synthetic-scan results to real degraded statements is an unverified assumption.
  - Quote: "The resulting images are realistic, noisy versions of clean documents, as evidenced in Figure 1, where we apply several Augraphy augmentations to a clean document image in order to mimic the types of noise seen in a real-world noisy document image from RVL-CDIP."
