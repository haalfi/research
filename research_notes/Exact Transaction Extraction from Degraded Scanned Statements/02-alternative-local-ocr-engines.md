# Track 2: Alternative local OCR engines: benchmarks on numeric/financial fields

Round 1 of the deep-research workflow, 2026-11-21. One reader agent per source extracted up to five claims with a supporting quote; the 25 highest-ranked claims across all tracks then went to three adversarial verifiers each (two refutations kill a claim).
Claims marked "not voted" are leads until verified; see the round 2 notes.

Search query: `PaddleOCR PP-OCRv5 vs docTR vs Tesseract vs Surya vs EasyOCR vs TrOCR benchmark comparison scanned invoices receipts numeric field accuracy digits amounts CPU offline word bounding boxes`

Rationale: Comparative evidence for locally runnable engines on degraded business documents, with attention to numeric-string exactness rather than general CER, CPU/offline install cost, and whether they emit word boxes compatible with a positional parser.

## S6: https://imagetotable.ai/references/receipt-ocr-benchmark-results

Source quality (reader's rating): blog. Date: 2026-08-12.

- **S6.0** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): On the 361-receipt SROIE 2019 test split, the engine with the lowest character error rate (docTR v1.0.1, 19.7% CER) had the worst regex-derived field F1 (7.7%), while PaddleOCR 3.7.0 (20.5% CER) had the best (32.5%). In this vendor benchmark, text-level accuracy ranking did not predict field-level ranking. Caveat: field F1 depends on one fixed regex postprocessor, so the inversion may reflect output formatting/reading order rather than recognition quality.
  - Quote: "docTR reads receipt text most accurately (19.7% CER) but extracts structured fields the worst (7.7% field F1), while PaddleOCR reads slightly less accurately (20.5% CER) yet extracts fields 4× better (32.5% field F1)"
- **S6.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): With default configurations and a fixed regex postprocessor, none of the five open-source engines (Tesseract 5.3.4, PaddleOCR 3.7.0, EasyOCR 1.7.2, docTR 1.0.1, Docling 2.119.0) produced a single SROIE receipt with all four fields (company, date, address, total) exactly correct; document-level exact match was 0 for every model.
  - Quote: "no model in this batch achieved even one fully correct document on all four fields (document-level field exact match = 0 for every model)"
- **S6.2** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): In this benchmark PaddleOCR 3.7.0 beat Tesseract 5.3.4 on SROIE on every reported metric: CER 20.5% vs 33.6%, WER 32.6% vs 56.2%, field F1 32.5% vs 23.2%, throughput 157.8 vs 54.4 pages/min (on an RTX 4090 GPU, not CPU). All engines were run out-of-the-box with no preprocessing tuning; the absolute CERs (20-58%) are far higher than usually reported for SROIE, suggesting the scoring (e.g. reading-order-sensitive full-page Levenshtein) inflates error rates, so only relative ordering is usable.
  - Quote: "Yes on every metric in this benchmark: PaddleOCR beats Tesseract on CER (20.5% vs 33.6%), WER (32.6% vs 56.2%), field F1 (32.5% vs 23.2%), speed (157.8 vs 54.4 pages/min), and cost ($0.080 vs $0.233 per 1,000 pages) on the 361-receipt SROIE test set."
- **S6.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The source attributes field-extraction failure on clean text to value-format mismatch with the extraction pattern (currency symbols, comma-decimal totals, multi-line layout), i.e. a downstream parsing error rather than a character recognition error. This is asserted as explanation, not shown with a per-error breakdown; per-field (date/total) numbers are not published.
  - Quote: "a regex pass over clean text can still fail when the value format (currency symbols, comma-decimal totals, multi-line addresses) does not match the extraction pattern"
- **S6.4** (tangential; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The benchmark is not independently reproducible at present: the code repository is unreleased, artifacts are available only on request, models were run with default configurations only, and results are limited to GPU (RTX 4090) on receipts (SROIE, CORD v2) rather than degraded tabular statements. On CORD v2 every model scored 90-95% CER, which the page attributes to language mismatch; that explanation is implausible for Latin-script receipts and more likely indicates an evaluation artifact, which lowers confidence in the absolute numbers.
  - Quote: "All models used out-of-the-box with default configurations. No fine-tuning, no custom language packs, no post-training adaptations."

## S7: https://imagetotable.ai/references/tesseract-vs-paddleocr-receipt-benchmark

Source quality (reader's rating): blog. Date: 2026-11-06.

- **S7.0** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): On the SROIE 2019 receipt test split (361 samples), PaddleOCR (run on an RTX 4090 GPU) had a lower character error rate than Tesseract (run on CPU, metadata version 4.1.1): CER 0.2045 vs 0.3347. The vendor itself states this is a comparison of two differing configurations, not an isolated test of model architecture, so it does not show what PaddleOCR would do CPU-only in an offline container.
  - Quote: "On SROIE, PaddleOCR has lower normalized text distance (CER 0.2045 vs Tesseract 0.3347) and higher postprocessed field F1. This is a measured comparison of two configurations, not an isolated test of model architecture."
- **S7.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Lower CER did not translate into high exact-field accuracy: with fixed-regex extraction SROIE field-value F1 was only 0.3254 (PaddleOCR) vs 0.2335 (Tesseract), and even with an LLM postprocessor the share of documents with all fields exact was 7.48% (PaddleOCR) vs 5.26% (Tesseract). Fields are the four SROIE fields combined; totals/dates are not broken out.
  - Quote: "For SROIE fields, PaddleOCR scores 0.3254 with fixed regex and 0.5810 with the historical LLM postprocessor; Tesseract scores 0.2335 and 0.4389. [...] Docs with all fields exact (LLM) 0.0526 0.0748"
- **S7.2** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The engine ranking reversed on a different receipt dataset under regex extraction: on CORD v2 (100 test receipts) Tesseract's regex field F1 (0.0818) exceeded PaddleOCR's (0.0154), although Tesseract returned empty output on 25 of 100 images and PaddleOCR on none. This shows general text-accuracy rankings do not reliably predict field-level extraction results across layouts.
  - Quote: "Tesseract nevertheless produces 25 empty outputs out of 100; PaddleOCR produces none. This is output coverage, not an API failure count. [...] CORD regex field F1 is 0.0818 for Tesseract and 0.0154 for PaddleOCR after corrected offline scoring."
- **S7.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The benchmark's CER/WER metric lowercases text and collapses whitespace before scoring, so it is insensitive to case and spacing differences and is not an exact-match measure; the page does not report any analysis of digit, decimal-separator, or punctuation errors, nor of degraded scans.
  - Quote: "The implementation lowercases text and collapses whitespace before scoring. Case-only differences and ordinary line-break/whitespace changes do not cause errors."
- **S7.4** (tangential; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Speed figures are confounded by hardware: per-request median latency was 670.9 ms for Tesseract on CPU vs 297.0 ms for PaddleOCR on an RTX 4090, and the page explicitly says the results do not establish a general CPU/GPU cost or throughput ranking. No CPU-only PaddleOCR measurement and no memory figures are given.
  - Quote: "Per-request p50 is 670.9 ms for Tesseract and 297.0 ms for PaddleOCR. A median is not a mean and is not a whole-batch timer. [...] These results do not establish a general CPU/GPU cost or throughput ranking."

## S8: https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.16203.md

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2026-05-05 (date printed in the arXiv v1 header; note the arXiv ID prefix 2607 would normally indicate July 2026, so the date is internally inconsistent; preprint, not peer-reviewed).

- **S8.0** (central; voted 3-0, confirmed): On ground-truth evaluation over five scanned-document benchmarks (50 sampled test images each; FUNSD, SROIE, EPHOIE, RXPAD, XFUND subsets), no single OCR engine among PaddleOCR, EasyOCR, Tesseract, Google Cloud Vision, Qwen3-VL-4B and GPT-4.1-mini was consistently best; the ranking changes by dataset. This supports the brief's warning that general benchmark rank does not transfer to a specific document type. Caveat: metric is ANLS on general text, not exact numeric/financial-field accuracy; the paper does not evaluate amounts, dates or punctuation separately.
  - Quote: "Table 3 shows that no single OCR engine consistently outperforms the others, indicating that performance is highly dataset-dependent and motivating the need for automatic OCR tool selection."
- **S8.1** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): On the SROIE scanned-receipt benchmark (closest of the tested sets to financial documents), Tesseract scored ANLS 0.38 versus PaddleOCR 0.51, EasyOCR 0.35 and Google Cloud Vision 0.56; Tesseract was lowest or near-lowest among the classical engines on most datasets (e.g. FUNSD 0.23 vs PaddleOCR 0.42). Caveat: default configurations, 50 images, whole-text ANLS, no degraded-scan or field-level breakdown, so this is a lead on alternative local engines, not proof for statement tables.
  - Quote: "Table 3: ANLS of text recognition with ground-truth. EPHOIE FUNSD SROIE RXPAD XF-ja XF-es XF-it XF-de XF-pt ... PaddleOCR 0.20 0.42 0.51 0.66 0.46 0.83 0.34 0.88 0.88 / EasyOCR 0.09 0.29 0.35 0.61 0.53 0.87 0.74 0.90 0.90 / Tesseract 0.02 0.23 0.38 0.52 0.45 0.79 0.69 0.84 0.84 / Cloud Vision 0.44 0.29 0.56 0.29 0.62 0.73 0.75 0.74 0.74"
- **S8.2** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A ground-truth-free engine-selection method (rank engines by how little an MLLM diagnose-and-correct cascade changes their output) reproduced annotation-based engine rankings with average NDCG of 0.9752 (FUNSD), 0.9679 (EPHOIE) and 0.9699 (RXPAD), but the authors themselves call the agreement 'moderate' and state rankings do not perfectly match. Relevant to evaluating gap months without a reference; however it ranks engines, it does not detect individual field errors, and it used MLLMs (two of four were hosted APIs: GPT-4.1-mini, Gemini-2.5-flash-lite) on NVIDIA A100 GPUs, which conflicts with an offline CPU-only constraint.
  - Quote: "Overall, we observe moderate agreement between the two regimes under NDCG evaluation. On FUNSD (Avg.d = 0.9752), correction-based rankings perfectly match annotation-based rankings ... EPHOIE achieves average NDCG values to 0.9679, while RXPAD reaches 0.9699, suggesting although exact rank matching may vary, the framework consistently preserves the most important top-ranked positions."
- **S8.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Vision-based re-OCR by an MLLM can add errors rather than remove them on text with domain-specific abbreviations: on the RXPAD prescription set the best correction strategy was text-only normalization, without the vision re-read. This is a published warning sign for LLM/VLM 'correction' of abbreviated merchant description strings, where no language prior constrains the answer (application to card statements is inference, not tested in the paper).
  - Quote: "In contrast, RXPAD favors text-only normalization (Dc + Dt), as domain-specific abbreviations make vision-based re-OCR prone to additional noise."
- **S8.4** (tangential; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The authors state MLLM-based correction adds latency and monetary or hardware cost relative to traditional OCR and should be optional, not a default pipeline stage.
  - Quote: "it introduces additional latency, because MLLMs are substantially slower than traditional OCR engines ... commercial APIs and large vision-language models incur monetary or hardware costs ... Accordingly, correction should be treated as a configurable option rather than a default solution."

## S9: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22726

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2026-10-15.

- **S9.0** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A character-level post-processing layer (CVM) that re-classifies each OCR'd character belonging to a predefined confusable pair with a pair-specific binary Random Forest on the patch cropped from the original image, and overrides the OCR only when the classifier disagrees with confidence >= 0.70, corrected 765 of 876 visually-similar-character errors (87.3%) while introducing 18 false corrections (2.3% of the 783 corrections applied). Full text read (arXiv HTML v1). Caveat: unreviewed preprint; test set is synthetic random strings in ten fonts, not scanned documents.
  - Quote: "of 876 VSC errors in baseline OCR outputs, CVM successfully corrected 765 (87.3%) and missed 111 (12.7%). CVM also introduced 18 false corrections, i.e., 2.3% of the 783 corrections it applied"
- **S9.1** (central; voted 3-0, confirmed): The reported gains were measured only on synthetically rendered, clean-background strings; the authors state that performance on scanned or degraded images is not established, and that some evaluation fonts overlap with training font families. The paper therefore provides no direct evidence for degraded scanned statements; applying it there is a task-specific hypothesis.
  - Quote: "The evaluation also relies on synthetically rendered strings with clean backgrounds; performance on scanned, photographed, or degraded images, where character segmentation is less reliable, remains to be established."
- **S9.2** (central; voted 3-0, confirmed): Only about half of baseline OCR errors were single-character substitutions within the confusable-pair set and hence within reach of this method (53.8% overall, 169 of 314; Tesseract 50.8%, 30 of 59). Insertions, deletions, transpositions and multi-character segmentation errors (e.g. 'rn'->'m', '13'->'B') cannot be detected or corrected because the method inherits the OCR engine's character boxes.
  - Quote: "Analysis revealed that 53.8% of all errors (169 of 314) were correctable, while 46.2% fell outside CVM scope"
- **S9.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): In the paper's baseline error taxonomy on non-lexical strings (Tesseract 4.0, EasyOCR, TrOCR), visual similarity was the largest error category (41.2%), with special-character misrecognition 17.1% and missing characters 13.3%; low-feature punctuation was typically lost or swapped (underscore and '-' replaced by space, '+' by 't' or 'f'). Relevant to the brief's concern about small punctuation marks, but measured on clean synthetic renders.
  - Quote: "Characters with limited geometric features produced high cross-category confusion, with underscore replaced by space, ‘+’ by ‘t’ or ‘f’, and ‘-’ by space."
- **S9.4** (tangential; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Averaged over ten fonts, character accuracy rose from 88.68% to 94.73% for Tesseract 4.0 (+6.05 points), and the paper reports EasyOCR as identical to Tesseract on every font because both engines allegedly made the same errors at the same positions. Credibility caveats from the full text: that identity between two architecturally different engines is implausible and unexplained by data; the TrOCR mean is stated as 82.63% (+8.87) in the text but 82.11% (+9.39) in Table 2; pair-level some homoglyphs (I/l/1 in Arial, Calibri) are declared indistinguishable. Treat the numbers as weak evidence, and not as an engine comparison.
  - Quote: "Tesseract and EasyOCR produced identical results for every font because both engines made the same misrecognitions at the same character positions on each test string"

## S10: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13523

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2025-11-17.

- **S10.0** (central; voted 3-0, confirmed): On 60 manually transcribed smartphone photographs of printed clinical reports, exact numeric-span accuracy did not track character error rate across engines: PaddleOCR had a lower CER (0.111) than Tesseract (0.189) yet lower numeric accuracy (0.674 vs 0.756), while docTR at nearly the same CER as PaddleOCR (0.108) reached 0.884. Numeric accuracy is defined as the fraction of reference numeric spans reproduced verbatim. (Cross-table comparison is my reading of Tables 1 and 2; the paper does not comment on the PaddleOCR inversion.)
  - Quote: "Table 2: N a c c N_{acc} across all models, with 95% confidence intervals. Model N a c c N_{acc} 95% CI docTR 0.884 [0.842, 0.921] GOT-OCR 2.0 0.832 [0.756, 0.900] PaddleOCR 0.674 [0.620, 0.726] Surya 0.821 [0.778, 0.860] Tesseract 0.756 [0.677, 0.831] InternVL-3.5-4B 0.927 [0.889, 0.959] Phi-4 MM 0.944 [0.907, 0.974] Qwen-2.5 VL 0.950 [0.914, 0.979]"
- **S10.1** (central; voted 3-0, confirmed): Even the best locally run compact multimodal models (Qwen-2.5 VL, Phi-4 MM, InternVL-3.5-4B) reproduced only about 93-95% of numeric spans verbatim (upper 95% CI bound 0.979), i.e. roughly 1 in 20 numbers was still wrong; no evaluated system approached exact numeric recovery on these degraded images.
  - Quote: "As shown in Table 2 , MLLMs achieve over 92% numeric accuracy, substantially higher than other systems."
- **S10.2** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The compact multimodal models that led on accuracy required a GPU and 11-67 s per image (measured on an NVIDIA A100 80GB; 17-47 GiB peak GPU memory), whereas Tesseract ran at 0.63 s/img with no GPU and docTR at 0.81 s/img with about 1 GiB GPU memory. CPU-only feasibility of the MLLMs was not tested.
  - Quote: "Compact MLLMs, deliver superior performance but require substantial GPU resources and longer runtimes (11–67 s/img), with Qwen and InternVL needing only around 17–18 GiB (feasible with 20 GB GPUs)"
- **S10.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): For Tesseract, PaddleOCR, Surya and GOT-OCR 2.0, per-document numeric accuracy fell together with overall CER/WER (e.g. Tesseract Spearman rho -0.594 with CER), so overall page quality is a usable signal for numeric risk in those engines; for docTR and the MLLMs the partial correlation was not significant, meaning numeric errors there are not predicted by overall transcription quality.
  - Quote: "most systems exhibit strong negative correlations between numeric accuracy and CER/WER, indicating that documents with corrupted numbers also tend to have higher overall error rates."
- **S10.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Generative end-to-end and multimodal models introduce failure modes beyond character substitution: GOT-OCR 2.0 had a WER of 0.395 despite a CER of 0.101, which the authors link to hallucination under noise, and MLLMs sometimes transcribed background text that annotators had excluded. The study covered page-level transcription only; table/layout extraction and word coordinates were not evaluated.
  - Quote: "the evaluated end-to-end model, GOT-OCR 2.0, showed significantly lower word-level accuracy in this setting, suggesting that some compact transformer-based unified models may remain prone to hallucination noisy conditions."

## S11: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24373

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2026-03-25.

- **S11.0** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): PP-OCRv5 (mobile variant) is a two-stage detection + recognition OCR system with about 5 million parameters in total; a higher-capacity PP-OCRv5_server variant also exists but is not the one evaluated in the paper. The paper reports no CPU latency, throughput or memory measurements (verified by full-text search: 'CPU' does not occur), so CPU-only Docker feasibility is implied by model size, not measured here.
  - Quote: "we develop PP-OCRv5, a highly efficient two-stage (detection + recognition) OCR system with a total of only 5 million parameters ... PP-OCRv5 is released in two variants: PP-OCRv5_mobile (5M parameters), which serves as the primary subject of this paper, and PP-OCRv5_server, a higher-capacity variant."
- **S11.1** (central; voted 3-0, confirmed): On the OmniDocBench component-level text-recognition evaluation (normalized edit distance, lower is better), PP-OCRv5 scores 0.067 overall versus Tesseract-OCR 0.324, EasyOCR 0.329, Surya 0.090 and GOT-OCR 0.077; Qwen3-VL (235B) is better at 0.026. All rows except PP-OCRv5 and Qwen3-VL are copied from the OmniDocBench paper, not re-run by the authors. This is a general page-crop benchmark (mixed Chinese/English, rotated text), not a test of numeric fields or degraded scans.
  - Quote: "As shown in Table 4, PP-OCRv5 achieves state-of-the-art performance among specialized OCR models on the overall metric (ALL_avg) with an average edit distance of 0.067. While very-large-scale multimodal models (VLMs) like Qwen3-VL achieve even lower scores, PP-OCRv5 demonstrates a favorable balance of high accuracy and practical efficiency for dedicated OCR tasks."
- **S11.2** (central; voted 3-0, confirmed): On the English-only column of the same table, the gap to Tesseract is far smaller than the overall figure suggests and PP-OCRv5 is not the best specialized engine: Tesseract-OCR 0.096, PP-OCRv5 0.058, Surya 0.057, GOT-OCR 0.041, Mathpix 0.033, GPT-4o 0.020. Tesseract's poor overall score is driven by Chinese (0.551) and rotated text (Rotate90 0.984, Rotate270 0.969), which are irrelevant to upright Latin-script statements. So the headline benchmark advantage does not transfer directly to this task.
  - Quote: "Tesseract-OCR - 0.324 0.096 0.551 0.250 0.439 0.328 0.331 0.426 0.117 0.984 0.969 ... Surya - 0.090 0.057 0.123 ... GOT-OCR 0.58B 0.077 0.041 0.112 ... PP-OCRv5 5M 0.067 0.058 0.076 0.103 0.070 0.081 0.095 0.072 0.012 0.058 0.139 [Table 4 columns: Params, ALL_avg, EN, ZH, Mixed, White, Single, Multi, Normal, Rotate90, Horizontal, Rotate270]"
- **S11.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The paper asserts that VLMs hallucinate text on low-quality images and lack accurate polygon-level boxes, and that PP-OCRv5 offers better localization and fewer hallucinations. These are stated as motivation and conclusions only: the paper contains no hallucination metric and no localization/box-accuracy experiment (the detection model was not the focus; evaluation is recognition edit distance and in-house recognition accuracy). Treat as the vendor's argument for a detection+recognition pipeline with word/line boxes (compatible with a positional parser), not as measured evidence.
  - Quote: "First, coarse localization: VLMs typically do not output accurate polygon-level bounding boxes, which is unacceptable for applications requiring structured data extraction. Second, text hallucination: In complex or low-quality images, VLMs may generate "hallucinated" text that does not exist in the image, compromising the reliability of the output."
- **S11.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): On Baidu's in-house recognition benchmark, PP-OCRv5 reaches 80.1% weighted accuracy versus 53.0% for PP-OCRv4 and 42.5% for PP-OCRv3; printed-English accuracy rises from 66.0% (v4) to 87.5% (v5), i.e. still about 12.5% of printed-English samples wrong by the benchmark's accuracy measure. The training set (about 22.6M samples) is dominated by Chinese, Japanese, handwriting and scene text; the paper reports nothing specific to digits, amounts, punctuation, blur or scanned-document degradation (full-text search finds no occurrences of 'punctuation', 'blur' or 'scanned').
  - Quote: "PP-OCRv3 42.5 12.5 22.2 72.9 77.0 ... PP-OCRv4 53.0 29.8 25.5 83.9 66.0 ... PP-OCRv5 80.1 41.7 49.4 86.1 87.5 [columns: Weighted Acc., Handwritten CN, Handwritten EN, Printed CN, Printed EN] ... Core capabilities in printed text recognition, especially for printed English, have also been markedly enhanced."
