# Track 4: Verification, confidence calibration and undetected-error control

Claims marked "not voted" are leads until verified; see the round 2 notes.

Search query: `OCR confidence calibration selective prediction undetected error rate human-in-the-loop verification financial data extraction checksum running balance constraint post-correction limits "word confidence" reliability Tesseract`

Rationale: Skeptical angle: how reliable OCR confidences actually are, what arithmetic/format constraints can and cannot detect (compensating errors, descriptions, punctuation), risk-coverage evaluation, double-entry/dual-read verification literature, and review-effort metrics for a flag-for-human workflow.

## S18: https://web.cs.umass.edu/publication/docs/2009/UM-CS-2009-031.pdf

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2009-06-30.

- **S18.0** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Thresholding Tesseract's built-in word confidence cannot produce an error-free accepted set: across 10 test documents (14,057 words, Tesseract word accuracy 58%-95%), no confidence threshold yields aggregate word precision above about 95%, and a threshold that happens to give an error-free list on one document does not transfer to others. (Note: 2009-era, pre-LSTM Tesseract; applicability to Tesseract 4/5 LSTM confidences is an inference, not tested here.)
  - Quote: "It is easy to see that there is no threshold of the confidence value that achives a word accuracy rate of higher than about 95%. Hence, there is no confidence level which can be used to produce clean word lists across all documents."
- **S18.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A conservative accept rule that abstains unless three conditions hold (OCR word is in the lexicon; no other lexicon word within Hamming distance 1; every glyph passes an intra-document consistency check against its 20 most similar glyphs by normalized correlation, theta = 0.66) accepted 843 of 14,057 words (6.0% average recall) with zero errors on the 10 documents. Zero undetected errors was bought with very low coverage.
  - Quote: "Our main result is that we are able to extract an average of 6% of the words in each document without making a single error. The total number of words in all of the conservative clean lists was 843, giving some informal support to the legitimacy of our bound, which suggests we would expect between 1 and 2 errors in a worst case scenario"
- **S18.2** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The analytic worst-case bound on the probability that an accepted word is wrong is 2e-3, and it holds only under stated assumptions (per-pair glyph confusion epsilon < 1e-3, independence of per-character checks, adequate document length, appropriate dictionary). The authors state the bound fails for heavily degraded or very short documents and, specifically, that a systematic confusion (one character consistently read as another) passes the consistency check.
  - Quote: "If a document is degraded or has a font such that one letter is consistently interpreted as another, then that error will likely pass the consistency check (e.g. ǫ will be very large). If a document is degraded or is very short, then δ may be much smaller"
- **S18.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The method makes no accept decision at all for non-dictionary tokens or for tokens with a valid one-substitution neighbour, and it excludes visually confusable class pairs from its distance measure. By construction it therefore gives no guarantee for strings such as numeric amounts, dates or merchant descriptions, where a single-character substitution yields another valid string (this last application to statements is my inference from the stated rules, not a claim made in the paper).
  - Quote: "First, if T is not a dictionary word, we discard it and make no attempt to classify whether it is correct. That is, we will not put it on the clean word list."
- **S18.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The high-precision clean set can serve as automatically labelled, document-specific training data to fix residual confusions: a linear SVM trained on 'o'/'c' glyphs from one document's conservative list corrected all four Tesseract o/c errors in that document with no new errors and no manual labelling. This is a single-document, single-character-pair demonstration. Relaxing the Hamming-ball rule (outside the analytic bound) raised recall to 18.9% (2,661 words) at the cost of 1 error.
  - Quote: "While Tesseract made four errors confusing “o”s and “c”s in the original document, all of these were corrected using our document-specific training set, and no new errors were introduced."

## S20: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17448

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2026-10-12.

- **S20.0** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A column/total sum check provably detects every single-cell misread but cannot detect any error pattern whose net effect on the sum is zero (compensating pairs, or values swapped/displaced between rows of the same column). The authors therefore describe a passed sum check as a certificate about the sum, not about the individual cells. Direct support for the brief's caution that a balance check does not guarantee each transaction is correct. (Full text read; Section V Proposition 1 and Section VII.)
  - Quote: "every pattern with exactly one nonzero component is detected, and every permutation of the entries of vc is undetectable. [...] The certificate is narrow: an EXACT column's sum equals a printed total [...] a certificate about sums and not about cells (Proposition 1)"
- **S20.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): High character-level accuracy does not imply correct field placement: on seven clean table panels (1214 numbers, 30 columns) the best open-source reader (docTR 1.1) reproduced 99.0% of digits but put only 83.7% of numbers in the right row and column and got 11 of 30 columns fully correct; Table I gives Tesseract 5.3 at 90.9% digits / 69.0% correct-cell / 3 columns, PaddleOCR PP-OCRv4 at 98.1% / 81.4% / 9, EasyOCR 1.7 at 77.3% / 41.6% / 0. Re-filing the same OCR words into an externally recovered column structure raised correct-cell rates (Tesseract 69.0 -> 83.4, PaddleOCR 81.4 -> 96.5, docTR 83.7 -> 97.9), i.e. much of the loss is layout/segmentation, not recognition. Domain is historical election tables, not statements; transfer is inference.
  - Quote: "On seven clean panels of election returns, the best open-source reader reproduces 99.0\% of the digits but files only 83.7\% of the figures in the correct cells and gets 11 of 30 columns correct."
- **S20.2** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Adding independently printed subtotals as extra constraints shrinks the set of undetectable errors: with group subtotals read correctly, a compensating pair of errors escapes only if both errors fall in the same subtotal group. The paper also derives that shorter verification blocks are safer (failure probability grows with block length R as 1-(1-theta)^R; optimal block about theta^(-1/2) rows at per-cell defect rate theta). Implication for statements (inference): per-page or per-section subtotals and running balances are stronger checks than one statement-level total.
  - Quote: "with the subtotals read correctly, a permutation is detected unless it preserves every group sum, and a compensating pair unless both members share a group."
- **S20.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): A three-way accept / flag / withhold gate driven by exact reconciliation to printed totals gave very low undetected-error rates on externally audited output: of 2201 Wisconsin columns the gate accepted 2042, flagged 118 and withheld 41; independent official returns confirmed 933 of 934 testable accepted column sums and 35 of 35 testable flagged totals; on three degraded 1888 pages all 51 columns released by the exact-total check were correct. Caveats: validation is itself at the sum level (the authors say it is 'blind to compensating errors within a column'), and the cell reader in this deployment is a hosted vision-language model, not an offline OCR engine, so the 99.2% correct-cell figure is not attainable under a local-only constraint as published.
  - Quote: "On 2201 columns of Wisconsin electoral data, the gate accepts 2042, flags 118, and withholds 41; [...] official returns confirm 933 of 934 testable accepted sums and all 35 testable flagged totals"
- **S20.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Reading the same cells twice with the same kind of reader does not yield independent errors, and an error shared by both readings is invisible to disagreement-based review and is caught only if the arithmetic check happens to expose it; an embedded PDF text layer used as an additional witness also slipped rows and misread digits and was treated as evidence only, never as ground truth. On degraded input (one-bit scans at 118 ppi) off-the-shelf readers collapsed at the field level: Tesseract 5.3 67.4% digits / 15.0% correct-cell, docTR 71.0% / 19.0%, PaddleOCR 60.8% / 13.9%, Amazon Textract 76.7% / 0.1%, with zero fully correct columns for all of them.
  - Quote: "a second pass adds evidence without making errors independent (Section VI-F). [...] A shared error [...] lies outside D and is caught only if uncompensated [...] The layer slipped rows and misread digits: a witness, never an arbiter."

## S21: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04117

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2024-09-06.

- **S21.0** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Error detection using OCR confidence is weak in absolute terms even in the best case: across 4 datasets x 6 engines, box-level error-detection F1 from a tuned confidence threshold alone is 0.22-0.57 for the commercial engines and DocTR (0.47-0.57 for DocTR, 0.47-0.53 for PaddleOCR), and the best confidence-aware BERT variant reaches only 0.34-0.86 on DocTR/commercial engines (Table 3, full text read). Integrating confidence into BERT 'mostly' helps, but gains are not always significant and a plain confidence threshold sometimes beats the learned model. Implication for the brief: per-word OCR confidence is probabilistic evidence, not a reliable undetected-error filter. Tesseract was NOT evaluated in this paper.
  - Quote: "The improvements in F_{1} score with ConfBERT are not always significant and the simple Optical Character Recognition-confidence-only baseline can outperform a more complex method (AWS+CORD, Azure+Private, Google+Private, Paddle+Private, Paddle+FUNSD). The improvements over the BERT model seem to be highest for well-calibrated Optical Character Recognitions."
- **S21.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Confidence calibration differs sharply between local open-source engines at default settings: Expected Calibration Error (10 bins) is 2.5-5.4 for DocTR versus 10.3-17.4 for EasyOCR and 11.5-22.6 for PaddleOCR across CORD/SROIE/FUNSD/a private set; commercial cloud engines are 1.1-3.6 (Table 2). DocTR is the only tested open-source engine with calibration near commercial levels (on CORD and SROIE).
  - Quote: "Finally, we also observe that the proprietary Optical Character Recognitions have a lower calibration error, although DocTR is showing similar calibration scores for CORD and SROIE as the commercial Optical Character Recognitions. We expect the calibration error to correlate with the usefulness of integrating confidence score into an error detection model as we present in the next section."
- **S21.2** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Line-level (coarse) recognition boxes degrade the usefulness of confidence for error detection: for EasyOCR and PaddleOCR, which output line/multi-word boxes, confidence-aware detection was sometimes worse than text-only BERT (EasyOCR+Private, Paddle+FUNSD), attributed to high ECE and to a low-confidence character not propagating to the whole-line score. Implication (inference for this task): confidence should be taken at word/character or per-field-crop granularity, not per line.
  - Quote: "Furthermore, the low granularity of these OCRs makes the confidence scores non-local, meaning that even if it had low confidence in a single character, this confidence might not be propagated to the predicted box spanning the whole line."
- **S21.3** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): On scanned receipts/forms/administrative documents with default settings, open-source engines had higher character error rates than commercial cloud OCR (CER: DocTR 3.2-7.7, PaddleOCR 4.3-8.9, EasyOCR 12.0-28.9 vs. AWS/Azure/Google 0.6-5.3), DocTR being the best open-source engine tested; qualitatively, errors concentrate on punctuation and special characters, especially for open-source engines. The authors caveat that part of the gap stems from differing bounding-box sizes and that this is not an OCR benchmark.
  - Quote: "A qualitative analysis of OCR errors shows a frequent occurrence of errors on punctuation and special characters, especially among open-source ones."
- **S21.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The paper frames the correct operating principle for automated decision pipelines as selective prediction (abstain when uncertain), and notes that language-model context in OCR is weak on numerically dense documents; prior work it cites found roughly 59% of OCR errors are real-word errors that dictionary checks cannot catch. These are framing/cited statements, not results of this paper's experiments.
  - Quote: "For industrial applications such as automated decision-making, prioritizing accuracy over coverage is crucial. It is better to hold off on a decision when there is too much uncertainty, than making decisions with lower accuracy."

## S22: https://online210.psych.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/PSY-210_Unit_Materials/PSY-210_Unit05_Materials/Barchard_abstract_CHB_2011.pdf

Source quality (reader's rating): primary. Date: 2011-09 (Computers in Human Behavior, Volume 27, Issue 5, pages 1834-1839; only the abstract page was reachable at this URL, full text not read).

- **S22.0** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): In a randomized experiment with 195 participants, visual checking (proofreading entered data against the source sheet) produced 2958% more errors than double entry. Relevance to the brief (inference, not stated in the source): a human-review step that only eyeballs OCR output against the scan is a weak control for undetected errors compared with two independent passes that are reconciled.
  - Quote: "Visual checking resulted in 2958% more errors than double entry, and was not significantly better than single entry."
- **S22.1** (central; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Visual checking was not statistically significantly better than single entry with no checking at all, i.e. a single human read-over added no measurable error reduction in this study.
  - Quote: "Visual checking resulted in 2958% more errors than double entry, and was not significantly better than single entry."
- **S22.2** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Study design: 195 undergraduates were randomly assigned to three methods (double entry, visual checking, single entry), trained, and each entered 30 data sheets containing six types of data. The population and material (trained undergraduates, psychology research data sheets) differ from financial statement transcription, so transfer to that setting is an inference.
  - Quote: "In this paper, 195 undergraduates were randomly assigned to three data entry methods: double entry, visual checking, and single entry. After training in their assigned method, participants entered 30 data sheets, each containing six types of data."
- **S22.3** (tangential; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): Residual entry errors left by visual checking were consequential downstream: 66% of visual-checking participants produced an incorrect coefficient alpha, sometimes off by more than .40, and a single entry error can nullify a moderate correlation or a significant t-test.
  - Quote: "For example, 66% of the visual checking participants produced incorrect values for coefficient alpha, which was sometimes wrong by more than .40."
- **S22.4** (supporting; extracted from the source by a reader agent; not voted in round 1): The authors recommend replacing single entry and visual checking with double entry or similarly effective methods.
  - Quote: "Single entry and visual checking should be replaced with more effective data entry methods, such as double entry."
