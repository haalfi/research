# Track 02: deep-research workflow, refuted claims

Claims that drew 2 or 3 refuting votes of 3. Do not cite them as stated. The workflow returned no unverified claims.

## R1 (vote 1-2)

The authors checked validity again by running a new exhaustive literature search about 4 years after the guidelines' last search. Of 668 references related to the recommendations, 69 (10.3%) were key new evidence, and these meant that 25 of 113 recommendations (22.1%) needed updating. Nothing in the guideline text itself signalled this loss of validity; it was found only through this external search. That last point is an inference that fits the abstract. The study supports the claim that a text loses validity silently when what it depends on changes. Source: abstract only.

Source: https://www.cmaj.ca/content/early/2014/09/08/cmaj.140547

## R2 (vote 0-3)

Cochrane's practice at the time (a practitioner report) and the panel's recommendation (a consensus standard): a review published without new studies loses validity silently, and the decision to update it is an individual judgment, not a mechanical rule. The panel calls mechanical rules unworkable and notes that Cochrane applies its update decisions informally and inconsistently across editorial teams.

Source: https://openaccess.sgul.ac.uk/108506/1/bmj.i3507.full.pdf

## R3 (vote 0-3)

Systematic reviews that are not updated lose accuracy as new evidence appears: conventional reviews are not updated, or are updated only intermittently, and between updates they are at risk of serious inaccuracy. This rests on Shojania et al. 2007's survival analysis (a cited first-hand study, not one run in this paper). It supports the statement's claim that a text can lose validity when what it depends on changes, with no signal in the text.

Source: https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10025041/1/Thomas_LSR overview paper final - revised.pdf

## R4 (vote 1-2)

Dialectic 'defeater' methods, used by 63% (12/19) of participants, have no clear stopping rule. FMEA, by contrast, has completion criteria. Practitioners name the missing stopping rule as a barrier, and as part of a 'confidence in confidence' meta-problem raised by 47% (9/19). This supports the statement's claim that 'review has no stopping rule'.

Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03657

## R5 (vote 1-2)

The documentation went stale without any signal in it. Developers did not remember which parts had to be updated, often knew it was outdated, and put off redocumentation in favour of features and bug-fixes. As a result they came to treat it as an unreliable 'dead artifact'. Causes come from developer interviews (qualitative, first-hand).

Source: https://wwwbroy.in.tum.de/publ/papers/2009_icpc_feilkas.pdf
