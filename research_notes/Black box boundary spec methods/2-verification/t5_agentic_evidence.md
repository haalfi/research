## Q1. What survives contact with the full texts? (claim-by-claim verdicts)

### Takeaway
Most § 6 figures exist in the papers, but **five claims are materially wrong or mis-scoped**. (1) Vericoding's 82.2/44.2/26.8% are *"model union"* rates (solved by at least one of nine LLMs). The best single models reach 67.5/30.9/17.9%. (2) The "8.6% semantic TLA+" figure covers only the 25 open-weight models. GPT-5 reaches 26.9% TLC passes and 100% syntactic validity in the same paper, so "at best 26.6% / 8.6% across 30 models" is false. (3) EvilGenie's "ambiguous" problems are 9 problems whose **tests reject valid outputs or contradict the spec**. That is a spec–test conflict, not prose ambiguity, and n = 9. (4) The AGENTS.md paper (latest version v3) finds **no significant effect** on task success, not a reduction. It appeared at **ICLR 2026 workshops**, not the main conference. (5) The TiCoder figure 48.39%→70.49% is from the 2022 v1. The peer-reviewed TSE version reports different numbers. Several grades should rise: Vericoding is now TMLR, the Gherkin study is FSE Companion '26, and When Prompts Go Wrong is ICSE 2026.

### Inferences
- *Inference.* The union/single-model confusion (T5-01) and the open-weight/proprietary confusion (T5-07) cut in opposite directions. Agents conform to fixed Dafny specs less well than the report says (about two-thirds for the best model, not 82%). They author TLA+ somewhat better than it says (GPT-5 26.9% TLC passes on 26 specs; Opus 4.5 16% on TLA+-Bench). The qualitative ordering (conforming beats authoring; Dafny > Verus > Lean) survives.
- *Inference.* EvilGenie (T5-16) should move from "ambiguity → hacking" evidence to "check stricter than or contradicting the spec → hacking" evidence, next to ImpossibleBench. One reading is directly relevant to the brief's "unspecified is a statement" (§ 3.2): a spec that permits several outcomes, checked by a test that accepts only one, invites hardcoding. That supports checks that accept *sets of outcomes*.

## Q3. Disconfirming search (b): is there a controlled study comparing spec formats as input to LLM code generation or agents?

### Takeaway
The report's literal claim survives only in narrowed form. **There is no controlled study comparing EARS, decision tables, or Gherkin vs prose for *coding agents*.** For single-shot *LLM code generation*, several controlled representation comparisons exist (2024–2026), two of them peer-reviewed: VeCoGen (FormaliSE 2025) and the Gherkin pilot (FSE Companion '26). None shows a structured or formal *written format* clearly beating prose on correctness. Formal-only input does worse than NL in VeCoGen, Gherkin does slightly worse than NL on average, and compact structured headers make no difference to constraint compliance. What consistently helps is *adding executable or precise content* (tests, contracts, model-layer signatures), not changing notation. So the evidence partly *contradicts the wording* ("none exists") but *supports the substance* (format choice is not settled by the literature, and no format is shown to help).
