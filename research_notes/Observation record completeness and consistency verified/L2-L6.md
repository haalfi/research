# L2 to L6: verified in full text

A verification agent read all five full texts. Copies are kept outside the
repo. Quotes and section numbers are the agent's; this file condenses them.

## L2. Correlated LLM errors

- **Citation:** Nee, Zhong, Ni, "Wavering Oracles: Selective Updating and
  Correlated Failures in LLMs and Their Implications for Scientific
  Workflows", arXiv 2609.11428v1, Sep 2026.
- **Grade:** A-pre. Full text: y. Data and code are only "available on
  request".
- **Setting:** SycoBench-600, four-option multiple-choice questions (555
  after exclusions) across 8 domains. The authors reanalysed the released
  logs of 7 models: GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Haiku, Claude Sonnet 4,
  Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama 4 Maverick and Mistral 7B. A local arm ran 3 small
  open models.
- **Method:** the error indicator is answer ≠ gold. ρ̄ is the mean pairwise
  Pearson correlation of errors, and N_eff = N / (1 + (N−1)ρ̄), "a compact
  equal correlation heuristic".
- **Results:**
  - Seven models give ρ̄ = 0.285, so N_eff = 2.58. The three local models
    give ρ̄ = 0.340, so N_eff = 1.79.
  - Answer agreement is 70.5%.
  - Plurality vote reaches 88.6%, below the best single model at 95.3%.
- **Not covered:** same-family pairs, and error correlation across repeated
  samples of one model. The repeated local calls agree on 99.2 to 99.5% of
  questions, but at temperature 0.
- **Verdict:** confirmed. The domain is multiple-choice benchmark
  questions, not review.
- **Transfer:** analogous. Agents were measured, as answerers.

## L3. LLMREI: LLM-led requirements elicitation interviews

- **Citation:** Korn, Gorsch, Vogelsang, "LLMREI: Automating Requirements
  Elicitation Interviews with LLMs", IEEE RE 2025, DOI
  10.1109/RE63999.2025.00013.
- **Grade:** A. Full text: y. Authors evaluating their own tool.
- **Setting:** gpt-4o conducted 33 interviews (17 with a long prompt, 16
  with a short one).
  - **The stakeholders were human students role-playing** two scenarios
    (hair salon, ski resort). They were not simulated by an LLM, contrary to
    the lead.
  - The authors wrote the ground truth: 8 and 12 requirements.
  - Elicitation was judged from interview summaries by two reviewers.
- **Results:**
  - "completely elicit up to 60.94% … partially … up to 12.76% (in total
    73.7%) using the short prompt".
  - "There was no significant difference … between the long and short
    prompts".
- **Questions outside the known requirements:** not measured against the
  oracle. "Context-enhancing" questions, which add information not mentioned
  before, were 10.4 to 15.3% of all questions. No count of requirements found
  beyond the ground truth.
- **Verdict:** figures confirmed. The setup was mis-described: humans played
  the stakeholders, and the figure is an "up to" for one prompt against small
  author-built oracles.
- **Transfer:** analogous to the agent's role in the design session (asking
  the questions). Agents measured: yes.

## L4. Query-Conditioned NLI

- **Citation:** Canby, Chen, Niu, Chen, Min, Aydore, Castelli (UIUC, AWS AI
  Labs), "Benchmarking Query-Conditioned Natural Language Inference",
  Findings of ACL 2025, pp. 14808–14835.
- **Grade:** A. Full text: y.
- **Task:** decide entailment, or contradiction, between two documents
  conditioned on a query. Strictly **pairwise**.
- **Datasets:** converted from SNLI, RobustQA (document inconsistency, 2,578
  items), RAGTruth (hallucination, 829) and FactScore. None are
  requirements, specifications or dialogs. The labels are GPT-4o
  conversions, with measured label quality of 76 to 94%.
- **Models:** GPT-4o, GPT-4, GPT-3.5-turbo, Gemini 1.5 Pro and Flash. The
  metric is balanced accuracy.
- **Results:**
  - **Document inconsistency (RobustQA): best 0.6811** (Gemini 1.5 Pro,
    few-shot). The few-shot average is 0.648, and the no-query baseline
    0.578 to 0.589. "RobustQA, in particular, has a long way to go: the
    ceiling is 93.94%, but our best method achieves 68.11%".
  - Hallucination detection (RAGTruth): best 0.8102 (GPT-4, few-shot).
- **Verdict:** the metadata is confirmed. The lead claimed no figures, and
  the figures found are weak.
- **Transfer:** analogous. Agents measured: yes.

## L5. RAG4C

- **Citation:** Liu, Tyszberowicz, Beckert, "Observable Consistency
  Checking across Requirements and Models", ENASE 2026, Vol. 1, pp. 50–63,
  SCITEPRESS, DOI 10.5220/0014719400004015.
- **Grade:** A. Full text: y. Authors evaluating their own tool.
- **Method:** GPT-4o with GraphRAG extracts "observables" and synonym
  groups. **Z3 checks consistency.** It belongs to the same hybrid family as
  SAT-LLM and ALICE.
- **"0.97 accuracy" is misattributed.** 0.97 is the P/R/F1 of
  **synonym-group construction** on 7 small synthetic datasets (Table 3). The
  authors themselves relabel it as "detects inconsistencies with high
  accuracy (0.97)".
- **Inconsistency detection:** a document-level verdict on 7 synthetic
  documents. RAG4C agreed with the ground truth on 7 of 7, ChatGPT and
  Gemini on 5. No precision or recall is reported.
- **Class balance:** every synthetic document is inconsistent by
  construction, so false alarms on consistent documents cannot be measured.
  The synthetic data was generated by ChatGPT-5.2, and the authors flag the
  bias.
- **"Industrial automotive case":** public deliverables of the EU project
  AI4CSM (151 requirements). "all tools reported the specification as
  consistent". It contained no inconsistency, so no detection was measured.
- **Extraction was measured:** observable extraction reached P 0.99, R 0.98
  on the synthetic data. This is the first extraction-step measurement found
  in this research, but it is on LLM-generated text, by the tool's authors.
- **Effort:** one expert took "more than two days" on the 7 small datasets,
  against about 2 minutes for RAG4C and 1 hour of validation. That is one
  anecdotal comparison.
- **Verdict:** corrected.
- **Transfer:** analogous. Agents measured: yes, for extraction.

## L6. ESEM 2026: LLMs finding missing elements in elicitation

- **Citation:** Kabadi, Wuntakal, Gutha, Bellarykar, Le, Thongtanunam,
  Treude, "Can LLMs Identify Missing Elements in Requirements Elicitation?
  An Empirical Evaluation Using Production-Derived User Stories in an
  Industry Setting", ESEM 2026, LIPIcs 82, Software Engineering in Practice
  track.
- **Grade:** A. Full text: y. **Own method and own data**: the practitioners
  are also authors, and they built the reference set.
- **Setting:** 3 projects. The reference set PROD was reconstructed from the
  live production systems (122, 110 and 220 stories).
  - The baseline is the analyst's stories from the same requirements
    document.
  - Claude Sonnet 4.6 ran once, zero-shot, per project, with a 10-stage
    prompt.
  - Matching used TF-IDF cosine with a threshold of 0.10, "selected
    heuristically".
- **Results:**
  - LLM recall 98.4, 94.5 and 79.5%, against the analyst's 37.7, 40.0 and
    72.7%.
  - LLM precision 46.5, 46.4 and 78.8%, against 97.9, 93.6 and 100%.
  - The LLM added about 3 gaps for every one the analyst found.
- **Caveats:**
  - The LLM produced about twice as many stories as PROD in two projects.
    With a 0.10 match threshold, recall is likely inflated (*Inference*).
- **Questions no checklist raises:** no. The method is a fixed, closed set
  of 10 dimensions: "No dimension is added, removed, or adjusted".
- **Verdict:** the wording is confirmed, as a paraphrase of the abstract.
- **Transfer:** analogous to T1's slot method: a checklist applied by an
  agent and scored against an independent reference. Agents measured: yes.
